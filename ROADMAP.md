# Wonderweave — Roadmap

**Status:** Proposed
**Governs:** The intended sequence of discovery and delivery. It does not authorize consequential scope changes or claim unbuilt work is complete.
**Last verified:** 2026-10-10

## Guiding approach

Prove the experience before building the machinery around it. Keep the first release small enough to finish and playtest, but crafted well enough to show why Wonderweave deserves to exist.

A phase advances on evidence, not enthusiasm alone. The roadmap is a living proposal and should change when playtests or research show a better path.

## Phase 1 — Define the first experience

**Goal:** Choose and design one compelling, self-contained mystery.

Work:
- identify the emotional arc: invitation, curiosity, struggle, insight, reveal, and satisfying closure;
- explore several concepts before committing to one;
- choose a concept that supports different strengths and at least one accessible alternate route;
- define the intended audience and safety assumptions for that specific experience;
- record factual sources, creative origins, and any cultural review needed;
- define what a successful playtest would demonstrate.

**Exit evidence:** A reviewed design brief, a complete puzzle/reveal outline, a hint strategy, and explicit playtest criteria.

## Phase 2 — Build a complete playable slice

**Goal:** Make the smallest end-to-end version that a real person can play without the designer standing beside them.

Work:
- produce the actual clues, assets, instructions, hints, and ending;
- ensure the experience has a clear start and finish;
- provide safe and accessible alternatives where the format requires them;
- keep implementation minimal until the play experience proves what infrastructure is needed;
- document the intended solution and likely failure points for testers.

**Exit evidence:** A versioned playable artifact, with its instructions and materials complete enough for independent testing.

## Phase 3 — Playtest and refine

**Goal:** Find out what players actually experience.

Work:
- test with people who have different levels of familiarity and different strengths;
- observe confusion, accidental shortcuts, inaccessible steps, dead ends, and unintended readings;
- distinguish a puzzle being difficult from a puzzle being unfair;
- revise and repeat rather than defending a flawed clue;
- keep a record of findings and why changes were made.

**Exit evidence:** Recorded playtest findings, resolved critical blockers, and evidence that the mystery can be completed fairly by its intended audience.

## Phase 4 — Establish the reusable pattern

**Goal:** Extract only the structures that the first successful experience demonstrates are useful.

Possible outputs include content formats, puzzle validation, accessibility checks, source/credit records, contribution review, and a way to connect one discovery to another. These remain options until evidence justifies them.

**Exit evidence:** A documented, tested pattern for creating and maintaining another mystery without flattening its creativity.

## Phase 5 — Expand the woven world

**Goal:** Grow from one strong experience into a coherent network of discoveries.

Possible directions:
- parallel mysteries with different entry points;
- cross-disciplinary connections and meta-mysteries;
- optional cooperative play;
- reviewed community contributions;
- multilingual and culturally contextualized experiences;
- a public interface if it demonstrably improves access and play.

Expansion is earned by the quality and maintainability of the experience, not by the number of puzzles published.
