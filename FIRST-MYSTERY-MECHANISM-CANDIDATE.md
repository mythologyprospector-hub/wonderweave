# Wonderweave — First Mystery Mechanism Candidate

**Status:** Proposed  
**Governs:** One candidate puzzle mechanism for The Unfinished Map; not approved or playtested.  
**Last verified:** 2026-10-10

## Design question

Does arranging the stations help players reconstruct a connected sequence of practical actions—and infer a plausible role for the blank center—rather than merely decorate a conclusion they could reach from any reading order?

## Mechanism

The board has four equally spaced station sockets around a ring and an unmarked center. The player must arrange Lantern, Seed, Bell, and River from three spatial clues:

- Lantern is opposite Bell.
- Seed is opposite River.
- Seed is immediately clockwise from Lantern.

Together, these clues establish one clockwise cycle, up to rotation: **Lantern → Seed → Bell → River**. The map must include a text equivalent and explain clockwise without relying on color or visual-only information.

The spatial deduction is an entry step, not the intended hard part. The intended value is what the order helps the player notice in the records.

## Record design

Each record describes a practical task and what is left available for another person. Across the clockwise sequence, the records form a progression:

1. Lantern uses chalk to identify a loose stone and returns it.
2. Seed uses chalk to mark planting rows, returns it, and leaves seed packets.
3. Bell uses a seed packet to repair a bare patch and leaves spare cord.
4. River uses the cord to mark a safe crossing and returns it.

The center is depicted as blank on the map; the records refer to materials being placed on or taken from a shared central shelf. The intended inference is limited: the center functions as a common-use resource point supporting work at different stations.

## Critical weakness to test

The sequence may still be unnecessary. A reader may infer “shared resource point” from the repeated shelf references even if the records are shuffled. If so, the spatial puzzle and the object trail remain loosely attached.

Do not treat the existence of a clockwise order as proof that order matters.

## Acceptance checks

Before artwork or playability claims:

- cold readers arrange the stations from the clues without coaching;
- readers explain the spatial evidence they used;
- readers identify the resource pattern and cite specific records;
- a comparison using the same records in a shuffled order helps reveal whether the intended sequence adds meaningful understanding;
- plausible alternate interpretations are documented fairly;
- readers distinguish the supported inference from unknowns about ownership, permission, and motive;
- the map and clues have accessible text equivalents.

The order-comparison is diagnostic, not a scientific experiment with a tiny sample. If the intended order adds no useful insight, redesign the mechanism or remove the ordering step. Do not rescue a weak connection with lore or decoration.

## Evidence boundary

**Shown:** spatial constraints, object use, objects left or returned, and a blank center with a central shelf described in the records.

**Inferred:** the center is intended to support shared access to useful materials.

**Not proved:** ownership, universal permission, whether every item is free to take, or the mapmaker's private motive.

## Current decision

This is a revised candidate intended to make the spatial step explicit and testable. It remains **proposed and untested**. Cold-reader behavior—not the designer's confidence—is the next evidence needed.

## Adversarial revision direction — not yet player-ready

The repeated phrase “center shelf” currently gives away the intended interpretation. A revision should remove that repeated explanation without removing the evidence needed to infer the center's role.

Candidate direction for the next draft:

- Keep the three spatial constraints, but treat them as a modest ordering aid—not as the mystery's achievement.
- Give each station record a local task and a concrete material transition: an item is left, used later, or returned. Do not repeatedly explain that the center is a shared shelf.
- Let the map supply the spatial evidence: the four station paths converge on an unlabelled central feature. Give that feature no name or stated purpose.
- Ensure the records contain enough traceable detail that reading in the constrained order reveals a progression of practical work, while a shuffled reading does not simply hand the same interpretation to the player.
- Do not imply that a sequence of useful actions proves universal access, common ownership, or the mapmaker's intent.

**Important:** this is a design direction, not a validated solution. Removing the shelf references alone would weaken the evidence rather than fix the puzzle. The records and map must be redesigned together. Keep the player draft marked as a draft until a coherent version exists; do not call this candidate playable or ready for cold readers yet.

### Desk-review gate

Before revising the player handout, write down what a reader can infer from (a) the map alone, (b) the records in arbitrary order, and (c) the records in the constrained order. The third case must contribute a specific, explainable insight—not merely make the same answer feel more obvious. If that difference cannot be stated cleanly, discard the ordering mechanic.
