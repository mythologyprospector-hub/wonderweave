# Wonderweave — First Mystery Mechanism Candidate

**Status:** Proposed  
**Governs:** One candidate puzzle mechanism for *The Unfinished Map*; not approved or playtested.  
**Last verified:** 2026-10-10

## Design question

Can a simple spatial deduction organize several small records into one connected discovery, so that the order of the records matters to the meaning of their evidence?

## Player-facing premise

The mapmaker left four stations around an empty center. Four records describe the stations and things that happened at the center. Three station notes give clockwise relationships. The player arranges the stations, then reads the records in that order to infer what role the empty center plays.

## Board and stations

The base map has four identical sockets around a ring, paths connecting neighboring sockets, and a blank center. It does not pre-place any station or mark a north position.

The four records are tied to these stations:
- Lantern
- Seed
- Bell
- River

The map needs a legible text equivalent and must not depend on color. Explain clockwise with a neutral example.

## Station relationships

Three records provide these links:

1. Lantern → Seed.
2. Seed → Bell.
3. Bell → River.

The ring closes the chain from River back to Lantern. The complete cycle is **Lantern → Seed → Bell → River → Lantern**, unique up to rotation.

The chain is intentionally simple in this draft. It should orient the player and establish the reading order, not pretend to be a difficult logic challenge.

## Connected object trail

Read in the station order, the records describe:
- chalk kept at the center, used at Lantern, then borrowed and returned at Seed;
- seed packets left at Seed, then one taken at Bell for a practical use;
- cord placed at Bell, then used at River and returned;
- a center shelf serving different stations, while the mapmaker leaves the center unnamed and unbounded.

The intended interpretation is that the center is a shared resource point rather than another station. The arrangement gives the object records an order, and the object trail gives the otherwise blank center a possible purpose.

## Evidence limits

**Shown:** the station relationships, object movements, center shelf, and blank center.

**Inferred:** the center is intended for shared use of useful materials.

**Not proved:** ownership, universal permission, whether every item is free for anyone to take, or the mapmaker's private motive. The reveal must not overclaim.

## Main design risks

- The station chain may be too easy to feel like a meaningful puzzle.
- The intended shared-resource reading may still be too obvious or moralizing.
- The object trail may not actually require the station order to make sense.
- The blank center may feel like an illustration of the answer rather than a discovery.
- The draft does not yet prove that the experience offers different meaningful ways to contribute.

## Acceptance checks

Before artwork or playability claims:
- cold readers can arrange the stations from the records without coaching;
- they read the object trail in the station order and can explain how that order contributes to the interpretation;
- they cite specific evidence for their conclusion and identify what remains uncertain;
- plausible alternate interpretations are documented and treated fairly;
- the reveal feels specific to this map and its evidence, not a generic lesson about sharing;
- the map and records have complete accessible equivalents.

If readers reach the intended conclusion without using the station order, the layers are not integrated enough. If station ordering adds no meaning to the records, redesign the mechanism. Do not solve a weak reveal with more lore or decoration.

## Current decision

This is a more integrated candidate than the prior draft, but remains **proposed and untested**. The next useful evidence is cold-reader behavior, not more prose polish or final artwork.
