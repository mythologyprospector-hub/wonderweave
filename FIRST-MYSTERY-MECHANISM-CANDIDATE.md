# Wonderweave — First Mystery Mechanism Candidate

**Status:** Proposed  
**Governs:** One candidate puzzle mechanism for *The Unfinished Map*; not a playable or approved puzzle.  
**Last verified:** 2026-10-10

## Design question

Can four small records, each understandable on its own, combine into a satisfying discovery without specialist knowledge, external searching, or a mandatory starting point?

The first attempt used four clockwise relationships to form a loop. That is logically sound but too simple by itself, and it does not explain the blank center.

The next attempt proposed four journeys converging on the center. This is testable, but **convergence alone is not yet a satisfying second discovery**: if the records merely tell players to trace routes that all end in the obvious center, the answer is mechanical and the blank's purpose remains unexplained. Do not mistake a valid diagram for a complete mystery.

## Current design hypothesis

The mystery may have two layers:

1. **Structural discovery:** evidence from the records establishes a relationship among the four outer stations.
2. **Meaningful discovery:** details distributed across the records establish why the center is intentionally left unnamed—or the player-facing setup explicitly makes the blank an invitation rather than promising a single hidden explanation.

The second layer must arise from evidence or honest framing, not a password, an arbitrary cipher, or the designer's preferred emotional interpretation.

## Player-facing setup under review

> The mapmaker's map has four named stations and an empty center. Four records were found among the mapmaker's papers. Each preserves part of a journey. You can inspect the records in any order. Work out how the records belong together, then decide what the evidence tells you about the empty place.

This wording deliberately does not promise that the records prove one exact reason for the blank. If the eventual evidence supports a unique explanation, the question can become more specific.

## The four stations

- **LANTERN** — lamp mark
- **RIVER** — wave mark
- **SEED** — seed mark
- **BELL** — bell mark

The marks are redundant visual identifiers, not color-only clues. Each has a plain-language label.

## Candidate structural mechanism: clockwise loop

Four local relationships previously proposed:

1. Surveyor's sketch: River follows immediately after Bell clockwise.
2. Gardener's observation: leaving Lantern and taking the next clockwise station brings you to Seed.
3. Ferryman's note: from Seed, the next clockwise station is Bell.
4. Mapmaker's annotation: River is followed directly by Lantern clockwise.

Together these imply the cycle **River → Lantern → Seed → Bell → River**, unique up to rotation. The clues establish the order, but do not establish north or any particular word for the center.

This mechanism is logically checkable, but it is likely too easy as the entire first mystery. It should be retained only if it supports a richer, fair discovery—not padded with decorative complexity.

## Candidate second layer: what the records establish about the center

A possible direction is to distribute complementary evidence about how the place is used, rather than having every record simply point at it. For example, different records might describe distinct actions that can occur there, while none claims the place as its own. The combined evidence could support the conclusion that the center is a shared place.

This is only a design direction, **not a settled solution**. The exact evidence must be written and tested. Labels such as Lantern, River, Seed, and Bell do not by themselves prove that the center is a commons, a meeting place, or anything else. If the records do not support a stronger conclusion, leave the interpretation open and say so plainly.

## Requirements for the next concrete draft

Before calling the mystery playable, create the complete player-facing materials together:

1. A legible map with explicit geometry and no hidden scale or orientation.
2. Four complete records, each with a distinct contribution and accessible text equivalent.
3. A step-by-step solution proving every factual inference.
4. A second-discovery argument that cites at least two independent details from the records, or an explicit reframing of the blank as an open invitation.
5. Three hints that clarify existing evidence without adding rules.
6. A spoiler-safe ending that provides a complete payoff without demanding one exact emotional response.
7. An alternate-solution review and a cold-reader test plan.

## Fairness and accessibility

- A player may begin with any record.
- Every inference must follow from information actually shown.
- No external search, specialist trivia, cultural assumption, arbitrary cipher, or brute force is required.
- Visual information has a complete text equivalent; color is never the only carrier of meaning.
- Direction terms and any map conventions are explained.
- No timed play, account, purchase, location tracking, or required public sharing.
- The solution guide must distinguish proven facts from plausible interpretation.

## Decision gate

This remains a candidate, not a playable puzzle. The clockwise loop is a structural baseline, and route convergence alone is not accepted as the second discovery. Do not mark the mystery playable, canon, or production-ready until the full materials exist, the solution is reproducible, alternate readings are documented, and cold readers can solve it without coaching.

If the evidence cannot support the promised reveal, change the reveal—not the evidence.
