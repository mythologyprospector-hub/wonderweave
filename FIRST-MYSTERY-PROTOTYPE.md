# Wonderweave — First Mystery Prototype Brief

**Status:** Proposed  
**Governs:** The design target for a first-mystery prototype; it does not certify that the puzzle logic is complete or playable.  
**Last verified:** 2026-10-10

## Working title

**The Unfinished Map**

## Design target

Create a short, self-contained mystery that a person can begin from a link or a printed page, enjoy alone or with others, and finish in one sitting. It should feel like a complete gift, not an advertisement for a future game.

**Target session:** roughly 15–30 minutes, subject to playtesting.  
**Format for the first test:** a compact digital document that can also be printed.  
**Account or sign-up:** none.  
**Required equipment:** ordinary reading and mark-making; no special tools.  
**Required real-world activity:** none.  
**Sharing:** optional and easy, with no public posting requirement.

These are prototype targets, not promises about the final game.

## The hook

A beautiful fictional map has a blank center. Four records left by its maker seem unrelated. The player can begin with whichever record catches their attention, find something meaningful, and gradually discover why the records belong together.

The first screen or page must communicate three things almost immediately:
1. Something is unfinished.
2. The player has enough information to begin.
3. There is a satisfying answer to discover—not an endless scavenger hunt.

## Experience shape

1. **Notice:** One clear visual question draws the player in.
2. **Choose:** The player may start with any of several records; no single sequence is mandatory.
3. **Discover:** Each record yields a verifiable observation or partial result.
4. **Connect:** The player finds evidence that the records share a rule or structure.
5. **Complete:** The combined evidence resolves the blank center.
6. **Understand:** The reveal explains why the clues were presented together and makes earlier details newly meaningful.
7. **Share or stop:** The ending is complete. A player may pass the mystery to someone else or simply enjoy having solved it.

## Requirements for the puzzle logic

The central mechanism is not yet approved. Before production, the designer must build the solution and all clues together, then verify:

- Each record has a distinct purpose and a fair path to its result.
- Players can start with more than one record without becoming stuck.
- The relationship among the records is signaled by evidence inside the experience.
- Combining the records is necessary or meaningfully more rewarding than solving each in isolation.
- The final result has one intended interpretation, or any acceptable alternate interpretations are explicitly accounted for.
- The solution does not depend on specialist trivia, cultural assumptions, external searching, guessing the author's taste, or brute force.
- Hints at three levels can restore progress without changing the rules.
- The full solution can be explained concisely after the reveal.

If those tests fail, redesign the mechanic; do not hide the weakness with lore or decoration.

## Accessibility and inclusion

- Every visual clue has a meaningful text description.
- No required information is conveyed only by color, sound, tiny print, or animation.
- Instructions use plain language and explain unfamiliar terms.
- Players can write, point, speak, or discuss their reasoning; no one interaction style is mandatory.
- The mystery can be solved solo, with a family, in a classroom, or by a small group.
- There is no speed leaderboard or penalty for using hints.
- Cultural references, if any, are contextualized and are not used as exotic decoration.

## Designing for word of mouth

The wish is global reach comparable to soccer's cultural reach. The first prototype cannot prove that ambition, but it can test whether the experience contains the beginnings of natural sharing.

Design for:
- **A clear invitation:** easy to explain in one sentence.
- **Low friction:** no account, purchase, download of a special tool, or personal disclosure.
- **A complete payoff:** players have a satisfying ending even if they never return.
- **A good share moment:** the ending offers a spoiler-safe way to invite another person to try it.
- **Group-friendly conversation:** players can compare how they noticed different clues without one person becoming the gatekeeper.
- **Cultural portability:** the core logic remains understandable across languages; any text-heavy version can be localized later without losing the mechanism.
- **Earned curiosity:** the ending may open a door to a wider world, but it must not withhold the promised resolution to force another click.

Do not add streaks, artificial urgency, social pressure, public scoreboards, referral rewards, or data collection merely to increase sharing. We want people to pass along something they genuinely enjoyed.

## Playtest plan

Test an early version with a small mix of people:
- someone who enjoys puzzles;
- someone who does not identify as a puzzle person;
- someone who encounters the materials without a live explanation;
- where feasible, people with different reading, sensory, or cognitive access needs.

Ask testers to think aloud if comfortable, but do not coach them through the intended path. Record:
- where they begin;
- which clues they understand or ignore;
- what they believe the goal is;
- where they get stuck and which hints help;
- whether the reveal feels fair;
- whether they finish;
- what they remember afterward;
- whether they would voluntarily share it, with whom, and why.

The goal is to learn, not to prove the designer was right. Do not infer success from praise alone; watch actual play and note friction.

## Exit gate before building a platform

The prototype is ready for broader playtesting only when:
- the complete player-facing experience and ending exist;
- the solution guide proves every inference;
- hints and accessible equivalents are included;
- the materials work without the author present;
- a reviewer can identify likely unintended solutions;
- the test plan and source/provenance notes are present.

A platform is not an exit requirement. A strong, portable mystery is.

## Immediate design work

Next, create a concrete puzzle-mechanism candidate with:
1. the final reveal written first;
2. the exact four records;
3. the intended solution, step by step;
4. the hint ladder;
5. a check for alternate solutions;
6. a rough visual layout.

Do not call it playable until a cold reader can solve it without coaching.
