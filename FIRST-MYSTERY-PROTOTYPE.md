# Wonderweave — First Mystery Prototype Brief

**Status:** Prototype brief paused pending a new mystery mechanism  
**Governs:** The design target for a first-mystery prototype; it does not certify that the puzzle logic is complete or playable.  
**Last verified:** 2026-10-10

## Working title

**The Unfinished Map**

## Design target

Create a short, self-contained mystery that a person can begin from a link or printed page, enjoy alone or with others, and finish in one sitting. It should feel like a complete gift, not an advertisement for a future game.

**Target session:** roughly 15–30 minutes, subject to playtesting.  
**Format for the first test:** a compact digital document that can also be printed.  
**Account or sign-up:** none.  
**Required equipment:** ordinary reading and mark-making; no special tools.  
**Required real-world activity:** none.  
**Sharing:** optional and easy, with no public posting requirement.

These are prototype targets, not promises about the final game.

## The hook

A map has four stations around a blank center. The player can begin with any record, arrange the stations from a few fair clues, and investigate what the records suggest about the empty center.

The first page should communicate three things quickly:
1. Something is unfinished.
2. The player has enough information to begin.
3. There is a bounded question to resolve, not an endless scavenger hunt.

## Experience shape

1. **Notice:** A clear visual question draws the player in.
2. **Choose:** The player may start with any record.
3. **Arrange:** A small spatial deduction establishes the reading order.
4. **Connect:** The ordered records may reveal a pattern in the use of shared materials.
5. **Interpret:** The player proposes a role for the blank center and supports it with evidence.
6. **Understand:** The solution distinguishes what is shown from what is inferred or unknown.
7. **Finish or share:** The ending is complete even if the player never returns.

## Requirements for the puzzle logic

The central mechanism remains unapproved. Before production, verify:

- The station arrangement follows from the stated clues.
- Each record is understandable without a live explanation.
- The order meaningfully contributes to interpreting the records, or the design is simplified to remove an unnecessary ordering step.
- The intended inference is supported by specific evidence and does not claim more than the records show.
- Plausible alternate interpretations are considered fairly.
- The solution does not depend on specialist trivia, cultural assumptions, external searching, or guessing the author's taste.
- Hints restore progress without changing the rules.
- The full solution can be explained concisely after the reveal.

If these tests fail, redesign the mechanism; do not hide the weakness with lore or decoration.

## Accessibility and inclusion

- Every visual clue has a meaningful text description.
- No required information is conveyed only by color, sound, tiny print, or animation.
- Instructions use plain language and explain unfamiliar terms.
- Players can write, point, speak, or discuss their reasoning; no one interaction style is mandatory.
- The mystery can be solved solo, with a family, in a classroom, or by a small group.
- There is no speed leaderboard or penalty for using hints.
- Cultural references, if any, are contextualized and not used as exotic decoration.

## Designing for word of mouth

The wish is global reach comparable to soccer's cultural reach. The first prototype cannot prove that ambition, but it can test whether the experience contains the beginnings of natural sharing.

Design for a clear invitation, low friction, a complete payoff, optional sharing, group-friendly conversation, and cultural portability. Do not add streaks, artificial urgency, social pressure, public scoreboards, referral rewards, or data collection merely to increase sharing.

## Playtest plan

Test an early version with a small mix of people:
- someone who enjoys puzzles;
- someone who does not identify as a puzzle person;
- someone who encounters the materials without a live explanation;
- where feasible, people with different reading, sensory, or cognitive access needs.

Do not coach readers through the intended path. Record where they begin, how they arrange the stations, what they infer, which details they cite, where they get stuck, whether the reveal feels fair, and what remains uncertain.

To test whether the order matters, compare the reasoning from the supported order with reasoning from a shuffled reading order. Treat this as qualitative diagnostic evidence, not a statistically powered experiment. If the order adds no useful insight, simplify or redesign the puzzle.

Praise alone is not proof of success; observe actual play and note friction.

## Exit gate before building a platform

The prototype is ready for broader playtesting only when:
- the complete player-facing experience and ending exist;
- the solution guide distinguishes evidence, inference, and unknowns;
- hints and accessible equivalents are included;
- the materials work without the author present;
- a reviewer has considered likely unintended solutions;
- the test plan and source/provenance notes are present.

A platform is not an exit requirement. A strong, portable mystery is.
