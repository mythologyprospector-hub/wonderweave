# Wonderweave — Game Canon

**Status:** Proposed
**Governs:** The intended player experience, design principles, and boundaries of Wonderweave. Owner-approved canon will govern project-specific decisions.
**Last verified:** 2026-10-10

## Purpose

Wonderweave aims to become a world-spanning, expandable game of discovery that people with very different ages, abilities, cultures, interests, and levels of experience can enjoy together.

Its defining achievement should be the quality of the experience: elegant design, meaningful discovery, careful craftsmanship, memorable surprises, and a world that rewards curiosity. Cryptography may be one tool among many; it is not the game's identity.

## The player promise

A player should be able to enter through something they already love, discover something they did not know, and find a meaningful next step without needing to become a different kind of person first.

The game should support several forms of contribution: noticing, experimenting, reasoning, making, researching, interpreting, collaborating, explaining, questioning, and helping others learn.

No single ability should define a player's worth. Speed, specialist knowledge, money, physical mobility, or access to expensive equipment must not become universal gates.

## Design principles

### 1. Many doors into shared discovery

Use varied forms of play: observation, physical-world patterns, language, stories, music, art, mathematics, science, history, logic, making, and social cooperation. These are possible design materials, not a checklist that every puzzle must satisfy.

### 2. Layered depth

A challenge may offer a welcoming first insight and deeper layers for players who want them. Difficulty should come from meaningful discovery, not arbitrary obscurity, missing context, or guessing what the author had in mind.

### 3. Fair clues and earned reveals

Players should be able to make progress through evidence, reasoning, experimentation, and useful hints. The answer should feel surprising in retrospect and fair in hindsight. A reveal should reward attention rather than merely confirm that someone already knew a secret fact.

### 4. Collaboration by design

Different approaches and partial insights should be able to complement one another. Collaboration must be an invitation, not a compulsory social burden; solo players should still have a satisfying path.

### 5. Craftsmanship over gimmicks

Typography, illustration, sound, writing, interface behavior, puzzle logic, pacing, and transitions are all part of the experience. Each should be intentional, coherent, accessible, and tested. Complexity is not quality by itself.

### 6. Wonder with intellectual honesty

The game may use fiction, mystery, symbolism, and imaginative framing. It must not misrepresent invented story as verified fact, or imply supernatural powers, secret surveillance, or real-world recruitment as a means of manipulating players. When real-world claims matter, make evidence and interpretation distinguishable.

### 7. Cultural care

Cultures are living and internally diverse, not puzzle props or collections of exotic symbols. Use reliable sources, contextualize material, credit contributors, and seek informed review when representing knowledge that needs it. Do not treat one culture's symbols as universal keys.

### 8. Accessibility is foundational

Design for varied reading levels, sensory access, mobility, cognition, language proficiency, equipment, and ways of interacting. Offer equivalent routes where practical; do not make accessibility a lesser version of the game.

### 9. Safety and privacy

No challenge may require trespassing, dangerous behavior, harassment, doxxing, surveillance of private people, disclosure of sensitive personal information, or purchasing something to remain eligible. Real-world activities must have safe alternatives. Experiences involving children require age-appropriate privacy and safeguarding by design.

### 10. Growth without losing coherence

Wonderweave may grow through authored mysteries, community-created work, real-world discovery, and larger meta-mysteries. Growth must not mean unreviewed content, incoherent rules, or an endless pile of disconnected puzzles. Quality, provenance, safety, and navigability matter at every scale.

## First proof of craft

The first milestone should be one small, complete mystery that tests the player promise before the project commits to a large platform or elaborate architecture.

It should demonstrate:

- multiple entry points or genuinely different ways to contribute;
- clear, fair clues and a usable hint path;
- at least one satisfying reveal;
- accessibility and solo-play considerations;
- evidence and source notes for factual material;
- a playtest plan that can reveal confusion, dead ends, and unintended interpretations;
- a complete ending that feels earned.

The exact story, puzzle count, presentation, and implementation method remain design decisions until the first mystery is shaped.

## Non-goals and boundaries

- Wonderweave is not merely a cryptography contest or a race for the fastest solver.
- It is not an excuse to make unsupported factual claims feel true.
- It is not a reason to collect unnecessary personal data or pressure people into public participation.
- It is not assumed to require a web application, mobile application, blockchain, token economy, or any particular technology.
- It is not considered successful merely because it is large, complicated, or technically impressive.

## Open design questions

These are research and design work, not questions the owner must answer before progress can continue:

- What should the first mystery make players feel and discover?
- What is the smallest complete experience that proves the game's distinctive quality?
- Which formats provide meaningful participation across ages and abilities?
- How should community contributions be reviewed, credited, versioned, and connected?
- What, if anything, needs to be online in the first playable version?
