# Wonderweave — First Mystery Playtest Draft

**Status:** Experimental draft for review and cold testing  
**Canon status:** Not approved; does not replace `FIRST-MYSTERY.md`  
**Purpose:** Test whether a small, complete deduction can feel like discovery without relying on an ornamental map, specialist knowledge, or an assumed larger secret.

---

## Player copy — The Museum of Small Things

### The empty place

The Museum of Small Things keeps objects that people nearly threw away. One morning, its curator found an empty place in a display case and a note:

> One object is missing. It belongs in the empty place because it is the only object that fits all three observations. You do not need outside knowledge. Everything needed is here.

The curator left four candidate objects on the table:

- **The shell** — a small spiral shell.
- **The key** — an old iron key.
- **The feather** — a grey feather.
- **The button** — a wooden button with two holes.

Three visitors had written down what they noticed:

**Observation A — The child**
> I picked it up and put it back. It has no moving parts.

**Observation B — The repairer**
> I checked the four objects. The missing object is not made of metal.

**Observation C — The gardener**
> I looked closely at the four objects. The missing object came from a living thing, but it is not a plant.

Which object belongs in the empty place? Explain how the observations support your answer.

Take your time. You can solve this by reading and comparing the descriptions; no real objects are needed.

### Optional reflection

After solving, consider: Which observation helped you most? Was there one you initially overlooked? You can keep your answer private, talk it over with someone you know, or share only the reasoning—not personal information.

---

## Hints

Reveal these one at a time, only if useful.

1. **Gentle nudge:** Do not search for a secret code. Compare the observations with each candidate object.
2. **Point to a feature:** Observation B rules out one candidate. Observation C narrows the choices by origin.
3. **Nearly there:** The shell and feather both come from animals, but Observation A distinguishes between them: a shell has no moving parts, and neither does a feather. So the observations as currently written may not distinguish those two candidates.

---

## Facilitator answer and design finding

**This draft is intentionally not yet a valid finished mystery.** The clues do not uniquely identify one answer: both the shell and feather satisfy all three observations. The hint ladder exposes the ambiguity rather than concealing it.

That is a deliberate first test of our design discipline: a pleasant premise and plausible clues are not enough. A puzzle is not fair if the intended answer is merely the one the author had in mind.

### Repair proposal to test — not yet applied

Change Observation A to:

> I held it up to the light. It lets light pass through its thin outer layer.

That still may be disputable for some shells and feathers depending on the specific specimens, so it is not a sufficiently reliable repair.

A better repair is to define the candidates more precisely and use directly stated properties:

- **Shell:** animal-made; hard; spiral; no moving parts.
- **Key:** metal; hard; has a moving part (its attached ring can turn).
- **Feather:** animal-made; soft; no moving parts.
- **Button:** plant-derived wood; hard; no moving parts.

Then replace Observation A with:

> The missing object is hard.

Keep Observation B and C. Under those definitions, the shell is the only candidate that is animal-derived, non-metal, and hard. The key is metal; the feather is soft; the button is plant-derived.

### Reviewer checks

- [ ] A cold reader can understand the goal without extra explanation.
- [ ] The defined object properties are visible in the player copy, not hidden in facilitator notes.
- [ ] The answer is unique from the information given.
- [ ] Each observation contributes to narrowing the choices.
- [ ] Hints help without introducing new evidence.
- [ ] A player can explain why the other three candidates fail.
- [ ] The ending feels like a small completion, not a teaser for an invented secret.
- [ ] Test with readers before promoting this into the official first-mystery proposal.

## Current conclusion

The first version fails the uniqueness test. Do not publish it as playable. The next iteration should place the candidate properties in the player materials, remove ambiguous everyday assumptions, and then be tested with readers who have not seen the solution. The map concept should return only if it makes a real deduction clearer or more meaningful.
