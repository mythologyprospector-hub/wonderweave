# The Commons

**A living gathering place where discoveries find their people.**

The Commons is a central idea in Wonderweave: a place where people can share observations, questions, clues, theories, works in progress, and discoveries—and where connections can form between people who might never otherwise meet.

It is not merely a message board. It helps discoveries find the people who can recognize them, build on them, challenge them, or connect them to something unexpected.

## How it might feel

One person keeps noticing a strange symbol while studying an unrelated subject. Another has a cipher that needs that symbol. Someone else recognizes that the decoded message points toward a fictional forbidden temple in an adventure. Their separate interests become a shared story.

The people do not need to have started in the same puzzle, know the same things, or understand the whole connection. Each brings a piece. Others may discover further connections later.

The example is illustrative, not a prescribed plot or fixed game mechanic.

## A world with layers, but no edges

Wonderweave may contain many overlapping layers of experience: simple play, mysteries, history, language, art, science, collaborative stories, research, personal quests, and worlds within worlds. These are not mandatory levels, a ranking of participants, or a complete taxonomy.

People should be able to enter from different doors, follow their own curiosity, stay with one small corner, move sideways, return later, or help create something that did not previously exist. A child's discovery and a specialist's insight can both matter.

No individual—including the original creators or an AI guide—needs to know the whole map. The world should be allowed to grow through participation. We can build reliable foundations and preserve what is known without deciding in advance what every connection means or how far Wonderweave can go.

There should be no predetermined final layer or artificial ceiling on what people may create together.

## What people might do in the Commons

- Share a pattern, symbol, question, clue, theory, experiment, artwork, or unfinished idea.
- Ask for help understanding a discovery without having to solve everything alone.
- Find people with complementary knowledge or interests.
- Offer a useful connection to someone else's investigation.
- Collaborate on mysteries, creative projects, learning, or research.
- Develop new challenges and invite others to explore them.
- Record discoveries in ways that let others build on them, with appropriate credit and context.

Participation should remain optional. People need not reveal their identity, disclose private details, socialize with strangers, or publish every discovery. Where possible, preserve both solitary and collaborative paths.

## Trust, safety, and care

An open-ended world still needs firm boundaries. The Commons must not be used to coordinate harm, dangerous dares, theft, trespassing, harassment, stalking, doxxing, surveillance of private people, exploitation, coercion, or unsafe real-world investigations. Redirect harmful ideas toward fictional, simulated, consent-based, or otherwise safe alternatives without turning safety into a prolonged debate.

Protect privacy, especially for children. Collect as little personal information as possible. Do not make public posting, direct contact with strangers, travel, purchases, or disclosure of identity a condition of participation. Distinguish fiction from real-world claims and evidence from speculation. Credit contributions fairly, and make room for disagreement without humiliation.

These principles are boundaries for participation, not reasons to predefine the entire world.

## Design posture

The Commons is a guiding concept, not a claim that a working Commons platform already exists. Its interface, moderation model, technical implementation, and specific mechanics remain open questions.

Start with small, real experiences and learn from participants. Build only what is needed to help discoveries meet one another. Avoid imposing a master map, rigid categories, or elaborate infrastructure before experience shows what is useful.

**We make room for connections. We do not decide in advance what all the connections must become.**
