# Wonderweave — First Mystery Solution Guide

**Status:** Proposed  
**Governs:** Hints, intended resolution, and review criteria for The Unfinished Map; not approved or playtested.  
**Last verified:** 2026-10-10

**Spoiler warning:** Keep this guide separate from the player-facing draft.

## Hint ladder

1. **Gentle nudge:** Start with the two pairs of opposite stations, then use the clockwise clue.
2. **More direct:** Place Lantern and Bell opposite each other. Seed must be opposite River, and Seed must sit immediately clockwise from Lantern.
3. **Nearly there:** After arranging the stations, track which materials are used, returned, left, or taken as you read clockwise.

## Intended resolution

### Spatial layer

- Lantern is opposite Bell.
- Seed is opposite River.
- Seed is immediately clockwise from Lantern.

These constraints establish the cycle **Lantern → Seed → Bell → River**, unique up to rotation.

### Record layer

Read in the clockwise sequence:

- Lantern uses chalk to identify a loose stone and returns it.
- Seed borrows the chalk for planting rows, returns it, and leaves seed packets.
- Bell takes a seed packet for a practical repair and leaves spare cord.
- River uses the cord to mark a safe crossing and returns it.

The intended interpretation is that the center is a **common-use resource point** supporting different practical tasks. This is an inference from the repeated use of the shelf and the movement of materials, not proof of a particular ownership model or social rule.

### What is shown and what is inferred

**Shown:** the station constraints; chalk is used and returned; seed packets are left and one is taken; cord is left, used, and returned; the center is blank on the map while records refer to a shelf there.

**Inferred:** the center is intended to support shared access to useful materials.

**Not proved:** who owns the land or materials, whether everyone is welcome, whether all items are free to take, or the mapmaker's private motive. Accept a reader's alternate interpretation if it fits the evidence and preserves these limits.

## Designer audit and test

**Current verdict: untested.** The spatial layer is a small deduction, not a difficult logic puzzle. The main risk is that the shelf references give away the intended interpretation regardless of station order.

With cold readers, record:

- whether they arrange the stations without coaching;
- what they conclude after reading in the supported order;
- which specific details support that conclusion;
- what remains uncertain to them;
- whether the spatial arrangement changed, clarified, or added anything to their interpretation.

As a diagnostic, ask a separate reader—or the same reader after recording their first answer—to review the four records in a shuffled order. Compare the reasoning, not just whether they guess “shared resource point.” This is qualitative design feedback, not statistically valid proof.

If shuffled records lead to the same interpretation with the same supporting reasoning, the ordering mechanic has not earned its place. Either redesign the records so the sequence reveals a real connection, or remove the spatial step and make a smaller, cleaner mystery. Do not add complexity merely to justify the map.

Do not create final artwork until the logic and text survive this review.
