# Wonderweave — First Mystery Solution Guide

**Status:** Proposed  
**Governs:** Hints, intended resolution, and review criteria for *The Unfinished Map*; not approved or playtested.  
**Last verified:** 2026-10-10

**Spoiler warning:** Keep this guide separate from the player-facing draft.

## Hint ladder

1. **Gentle nudge:** Follow the station notes to arrange the records. Then read them in that order and track each object.
2. **More direct:** The chalk, seed packets, and cord each appear in more than one record. Notice what is left, taken, used, and returned.
3. **Nearly there:** Useful things are kept at the center shelf, used at different stations, and returned or replenished. What kind of place would make that pattern useful?

## Intended resolution

### Structural layer

The station notes establish:

1. Lantern → Seed.
2. Seed → Bell.
3. Bell → River.

The four stations form a ring, so River is followed by Lantern. The clockwise cycle is **Lantern → Seed → Bell → River → Lantern**, unique up to rotation.

### Evidence layer

Reading the records in that order reveals a connected sequence of shared resources:

- Chalk is kept at the center, used at Lantern, then borrowed at Seed and returned.
- Seed packets are left at Seed, then one is taken at Bell for a practical use.
- Cord is placed at Bell, then taken at River for a practical use and returned.
- The center shelf supports actions at different stations; the mapmaker draws no name or boundary around the center.

The intended interpretation is that the center functions as a **shared resource point**, not simply another station: useful materials are made available there, used in different places, and returned or replenished. The order helps the player see the object trail as one connected system rather than four unrelated anecdotes.

### What is shown and what is inferred

**Shown:** the station links; the chalk's use and return; seed packets being left and one taken; cord being left, used, and returned; the unlabelled, unbounded center.

**Inferred:** the center is intended to support shared use of useful materials.

**Not proved:** who owns the land, whether absolutely everyone is welcome, whether every item is free for anyone to take, or the mapmaker's private motive. The solution must preserve this uncertainty.

## Designer's pre-playtest audit — not player-facing

**Current verdict: stronger integration, still untested.** This is a design hypothesis, not a playtest result.

The station sequence now orders the object trail, so the two layers reinforce one another. However, the station chain may still be too easy, and the shared-resource interpretation may still be obvious or feel like a moral lesson. Do not assume integration alone makes the experience satisfying.

Cold readers should be asked to explain:
- how the station notes establish the ring order;
- how the order changes or clarifies their reading of the objects;
- which details support a shared-resource interpretation;
- what other interpretations remain possible;
- what, if anything, felt surprising or satisfying.

If readers infer the intended conclusion without using the station order, the layers remain insufficiently integrated. If they solve the order but see no new meaning in the records, the evidence layer has failed. If they only repeat a general moral about sharing, redesign the reveal rather than adding decorative complexity.

Create final map and station-card artwork only after the logic and text survive cold-reader review.
