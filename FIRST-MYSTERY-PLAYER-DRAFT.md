## The Unfinished Map

The mapmaker left a map with four stations around an empty center. Four short records describe work done at the stations and materials left for others.

You may begin with any record. First arrange the stations using the notes. Then read the records clockwise.

**What role might the empty center play in the life of these four stations?** Use details from the records to support your answer, and note what the records do not tell you.

## The map

The map has four equally spaced sockets around a ring. The center is blank. Paths connect neighboring stations around the ring.

    [       ]
   /         \
[       ]  ○  [       ]
   \         /
    [       ]

The brackets are station sockets; the circle is the blank center. **Clockwise** means moving around the ring in the same direction as the hands of a clock: from 12 to 3, then 6, then 9, and back to 12.

## The four records

### Record A — Lantern Station

**Map note:** Lantern is opposite Bell.

A loose stone made the path uneven. I used chalk to mark it for repair, then returned the chalk to the shelf at the center.

### Record B — Seed Station

**Map note:** Seed is opposite River.

I borrowed chalk from the center shelf to mark three planting rows, then returned it. I left a small bundle of seed packets on the shelf for whoever needed them.

### Record C — Bell Station

**Map note:** Seed comes immediately clockwise from Lantern.

I took one seed packet from the center shelf and used it to fill a bare patch beside the path. Before leaving, I placed a coil of spare cord on the shelf.

### Record D — River Station

I took the cord from the center shelf to mark a safe crossing, then returned it. The mapmaker drew the four stations and their connecting paths but left the center without a name or boundary.

## Before you finish

- Which clockwise arrangement satisfies all three map notes?
- What changes, or becomes clearer, when you read the records in that order?
- Which details support your interpretation of the center?
- What remains uncertain?

There is no penalty for taking your time or changing your mind. You may solve this alone or discuss it with others.
