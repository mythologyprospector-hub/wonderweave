## The Unfinished Map

The mapmaker left a map with an empty center, four unmarked places around it, and four station cards. Three of the records describe relationships between stations. All four tell something that happened at the center.

You can read the records in any order.

Your task is to arrange the four stations around the ring. Then look again at the records: **what, if anything, can you infer about the empty center?**

## The map

The map has four equally spaced sockets around a ring. The center is blank.

```text
                 [       ]

        [       ]    ○    [       ]

                 [       ]
```

The circle marks the empty center; the four brackets are the station sockets.

**Clockwise** means moving around the ring in the same direction as the hands of a clock: from 12 to 3, then 6, then 9, and back to 12.

## The station cards

Place each card in one socket. Use every card once.

- **LANTERN** — lamp mark
- **RIVER** — wave mark
- **SEED** — seed mark
- **BELL** — bell mark

## The four records

### Record A — Surveyor's sketch

**Station note:** River comes immediately after Bell when moving clockwise.

**Dawn, first day:** A piece of chalk lay at the center. I used it to mark a loose stone on the river path, then put it back where I found it.

### Record B — Gardener's field note

**Station note:** Moving clockwise from Lantern brings you directly to Seed.

**Midday, first day:** I borrowed the chalk to mark three planting rows and returned it. I left a small bundle of seed packets on the stone shelf at the center.

### Record C — Ferryman's travel log

**Station note:** Moving clockwise from Seed brings you directly to Bell.

**Sunset, first day:** I took one seed packet from the bundle to repair a bare patch on the riverbank. I left a coil of spare cord on the stone shelf before heading home.

### Record D — Mapmaker's annotation

**Morning, second day:** I used the cord to mark a safe crossing and returned it to the stone shelf. I drew four empty sockets and the paths between them, leaving the center without a label or boundary.

## Before you finish

- What order did you place the stations in? What evidence supports it?
- Which details from different records seem connected?
- What does the evidence support—and what does it leave uncertain?

There is no penalty for taking your time or changing your mind. You may solve this alone or discuss it with others.

## Guide-only material — do not show before a player asks for help

### Hint 1

Use the station notes to place the cards. Then compare what each record says happened at the center.

### Hint 2

Track the chalk, seed packets, and cord across the records.

### Hint 3

Useful things are left at the center, used by different people, and returned. What might that suggest about how the place is used?

### Intended resolution

**Station arrangement:** The notes give Lantern → Seed, Seed → Bell, and Bell → River. Since the four positions form a ring, River leads back to Lantern. The cycle is Lantern → Seed → Bell → River → Lantern, unique up to rotation.

**Evidence at the center:** Chalk is used and returned, seed packets are left and one is taken for a practical purpose, and cord is left, used, and returned. The center has a shelf, and the mapmaker leaves it without a label or boundary. Together, these details support the interpretation that the center functions as a shared-use place where useful things can be left and taken.

**Limits of the evidence:** The records do not prove who owns the land, whether absolutely everyone is welcome, or what the mapmaker privately intended. The solution should explain the shared-use interpretation as a supported inference, not an established fact.

## Review notes — not player-facing

- This is an assembled text draft, not a designed visual artifact or a tested game.
- Check whether the station arrangement feels like a small discovery rather than a worksheet.
- Check whether the shared-use interpretation is discovered from the records rather than guessed from the question.
- Ask cold readers to cite the details that support their interpretation and to name what remains uncertain.
- If readers only repeat a general moral about sharing, the second layer has not worked.
- Create the final map and station-card artwork only after the text and logic survive cold-reader review.
